This template is designed to help you capture notes in real time as you read, ensuring you don't miss the specific Health IT nuances (such as interoperability or data privacy) that editors look for.

------

## Peer Review Drafting Template

**1. Quick Summary (The "Elevator Pitch")**

- **What was done?** (e.g., *A randomized controlled trial of a new diabetes mHealth app...*)
- **Who was studied?** (e.g., *...among 150 rural patients over 6 months.*)
- **The "So What?":** (e.g., *The study finds a significant drop in HbA1c but suffers from high attrition.*)

**2. Major Strengths**

- *Example: "The use of the [Specific Framework, e.g., RE-AIM] provides a strong theoretical backbone for the implementation analysis."*
- *Example: "The integration with live EHR data is a technical highlight that adds real-world validity."*

**3. Major Concerns (The "Fatal Flaws")**

- **Methodology:** Is the "n" too small? Is there a selection bias?
- **Technical Rigor:** Did they explain the data security (HIPAA/GDPR)? Is the tech stack described well enough to replicate?
- **Results vs. Claims:** Do the numbers actually support the conclusion? (Check for "p-hacking" or ignoring negative data).

**4. Minor Points (The "Easy Fixes")**

- **Clarity:** "Figure 1 is blurry; suggest a high-res SVG."
- **Jargon:** "Define 'interoperability' in the context of this specific hospital system."
- **References:** "Add more recent 2023/2024 citations regarding AI ethics in healthcare."

**5. Confidential Recommendation (For the Editor Only)**

- **Verdict:** [Accept / Minor Revision / Major Revision / Reject]
- **The "Why":** (e.g., *"While the tech is cool, the clinical outcomes are non-significant, making it a better fit for a 'Technical Note' than a full 'Original Research' article."*)
