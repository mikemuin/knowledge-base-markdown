CONSORT and STARD are standardized reporting guidelines used in healthcare research to ensure that study results are reported clearly, completely, and transparently. These guidelines help peer reviewers and editors evaluate the validity and risk of bias in a manuscript. [1, 2, 3, 4]

## CONSORT (Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials)

- **Purpose**: Designed specifically for **randomized controlled trials (RCTs)**.
- **Structure**: Consists of a **checklist** (updated to 30 items in the 2025 version) and a **flow diagram**.
- **Key Focus**: It guides authors on reporting essential details about the trial design, participant flow, randomization methods, and statistical analysis.
- **Use Case in Health IT**: Reviewers use CONSORT to check if a trial for a new health app or clinical tool was conducted with proper blinding and randomization. [2, 5, 6, 7, 8]

## STARD (Standards for Reporting Diagnostic Accuracy Studies)

- **Purpose**: Aimed at studies evaluating the **accuracy of diagnostic tests**.
- **Structure**: Includes a **30-item checklist** and a **flow diagram** specifically for diagnostic research.
- **Key Focus**: It ensures authors report how they selected participants, the reference standard used for comparison, and the clinical information available to test interpreters.
- **Use Case in Health IT**: Reviewers use STARD when evaluating manuscripts about new AI-based diagnostic algorithms or imaging software to ensure the "ground truth" (reference standard) was applied correctly. [3, 4, 9, 10, 11]

| Feature [2, 3, 4, 7, 10, 12, 13] | CONSORT                                      | STARD                                       |
| :------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------ |
| **Full Name**                    | Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials   | Standards for Reporting Diagnostic Accuracy |
| **Primary Study Type**           | Randomized Controlled Trials (RCTs)          | Diagnostic Accuracy Studies                 |
| **Checklist Size**               | 30 items (CONSORT 2025)                      | 30 items (STARD 2015)                       |
| **Main Goal**                    | Transparent reporting of trial interventions | Evaluating test performance and bias        |

Both guidelines are hosted by the [EQUATOR Network](https://www.equator-network.org/), a global resource for health research reporting standards. [14, 15]
