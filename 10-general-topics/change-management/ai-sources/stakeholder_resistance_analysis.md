# Stakeholder Resistance Analysis Framework

Source: Gemini

What you’ve described is a **Stakeholder Mapping** or **Stakeholder Analysis** framework. While it doesn't have one single "trademarked" name, it is most commonly referred to as the **Attitude/Support Continuum** or a **Stakeholder Resistance Scale**.

It is a tool used in project management and change management to categorize individuals based on their emotional and behavioral commitment to a project.

------

### Breaking Down the Levels

This specific version of the scale is often used to move stakeholders from the bottom (Resistance) to the top (Active Support).

| **Level**              | **Characteristic**       | **Typical Behavior**                                         |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Active Support**     | **Champions**            | Proactively promotes the project; solves problems before they reach the lead. |
| **Passive Support**    | **Happily Allow**        | Agrees with the goal but won't "work" for it unless asked.   |
| **Neutral**            | **The Middle Ground**    | Often where "Attend meetings (no contribution)" sits. They are waiting to see who wins. |
| **Passive Resistance** | **Delayed Coordination** | "Slow-rolling" tasks, missing deadlines, or being "too busy" to help. |
| **Active Resistance**  | **Loud Anti-Project**    | Vocal criticism, attempting to divert funding, or actively recruiting others to oppose. |

------

### Why the "Source of Champions" Note?

Your mention of **"Happily Allow Things to Happen (Source of Champions)"** is a specific insight from change management theory. It suggests that you don't always need to convert your "Enemies" into "Friends." Instead, it is often more efficient to:

1. **Neutralize** the Active Resistors (get them to move to Passive).
2. **Activate** the Passive Supporters (turn the "Happily Allowers" into "Champions").

### Similar Frameworks

If you are looking for more formal versions of this, you might want to look into:

- **The Diffusion of Innovation (Rogers):** Categorizes people as Innovators, Early Adopters, Laggards, etc.
- **The Support-Influence Matrix:** Maps these attitudes against how much power the person actually has.
- **ADKAR Model:** A goal-oriented change management model that tracks Awareness, Desire, Knowledge, Ability, and Reinforcement.
