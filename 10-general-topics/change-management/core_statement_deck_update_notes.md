## Slide 5 — The Core Statement

### Replace Core Statement

Replace the previous Core Statement with:

> Change management is the disciplined management of failure across People, Process, and Technology, from first decision to sustained adoption, moving the organization from the current state to the future state.

### Replace Slide Subtext

Replace the Slide 5 subtext with:

> This is not a softer project-management slogan. It is an operating definition. The work is to surface failure early, assign ownership, and keep control until adoption is real, because in Health IT the movement from current state to future state is where clinical continuity, trust, and data integrity are most exposed.

### Replace Voiceover Explanation

Replace the explanatory narration after the Core Statement with:

> There are two phrases to hold onto.
>
> "From first decision to sustained adoption" means change management starts when the real choices are being made, and it does not stop just because the system is live.
>
> "Moving the organization from current state to future state" means the real goal is not launch. The real goal is changing how work actually gets done.
>
> This is not a softer project-management slogan. It is an operating definition.
>
> The work is to surface failure early, assign ownership, and keep control until the new way of working holds under real operating conditions, because in Health IT the movement from current state to future state is where clinical continuity, trust, and data integrity are most exposed.

## Slide 6 — Governing Principles. Three Layers.

### Update Governing Principle 1

Replace:

> Start on day one, not day done — change management runs from the first planning meeting, not after delivery

With:

> Start on day one, not day done — change management starts with the first real decision, not after delivery

Rationale: this aligns Slide 6 with the revised Core Statement phrase "from first decision to sustained adoption." In Health IT, the first consequential change-management failure often occurs before formal planning: vendor selection assumptions, workflow scope, governance design, integration boundaries, and ownership decisions.

## Slide 7 — Identify. Question. Implement.

### Replace Slide Subtext

Replace:

> This is not a one-time exercise. It runs from the first planning meeting through go-live and beyond.

With:

> This is not a one-time exercise. It runs from the first decision through sustained adoption.

### Replace Voiceover Closing Line

Replace:

> The point is simple: this is not a workshop exercise. It repeats at every major milestone, through go-live, and into sustainment.

With:

> The point is simple: this is not a workshop exercise. It repeats at every major milestone, through go-live, and until adoption is sustained.

Rationale: this keeps the loop tied to adoption as the end condition, not go-live and not a vague sustainment phase.

## Summary Of Intent

The revised wording shifts the deck's spine in three useful ways:

- It keeps "disciplined management of failure" as the framework's core intellectual claim.
- It replaces project-timeline language with adoption-timeline language.
- It makes change management the active mechanism for moving the organization from current state to future state.
