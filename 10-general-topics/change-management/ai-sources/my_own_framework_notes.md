# My Own Framework

I want to build a change management framework for Health IT. Help me organize my thoughts

I look at change management through the lens of failure management — the ability to identify top risks, prevent, detect, and respond, and determine who the owners are.

I want an overlay of PPT — People, Process, Technology — because it makes change management real. These are the things we need to change.

“Change management is the management of failure across people, process, and technology.”

## Notes:

- Failure is the default. Success is what you engineer against it.
- Success is engineered (driven). It is not the default.
- It is not something you do within project management. It is something you do CONCURRENT with project execution. It is navigating the environment where the project lives. It is staying the course.
- Most practitioners treat change management as something you layer on top of a project. You deliver the system, then you "do" change management. Your principle implicitly rejects that. *Disciplined* means it runs **concurrent with project execution**, not after it. That's a significant organizational commitment, and it's where most Health IT programs quietly abandon the framework mid-stream.

### Core Principle

> **Change management is the disciplined management of failure within People, Process, and Technology during  project execution.**

### Strategic Questions:

- Where can we expect failure? (Reframing question: If this project fails, where would it fail?)
  - "What does failure look like before anyone admits it's happening?"

- What are the sociotechnical dynamics at play?
- Who needs to decide?

(I feel the last 2 are just for People Component. Maybe Process too.)

### Structure of the Model (Reverse PPT)

You operate through three lenses:

- **People → Behavior system** (from start to finish, engagement, ownership, adoption)
- **Process → Execution system** (what is to what it should be, current to future state)
- **Technology → Enablement system** (what tech is needed to succed)

Within each lens, you run the same logic:

> **Define → Anticipate → Prevent → Detect → Respond**

### PEOPLE Layer (Behavior System)

- Hardest to manage
- Most sensitive
- Many hesitate to act

### PROCESS Layer (Execution System)

- The easiest to underestimate
- **The hybrid state is the most dangerous operational condition in any Health IT implementation.** It is the period where some staff are on the new process, some are on the old, some documentation is in the system, some is on paper, and no one has a complete picture of the patient.

### TECHNOLOGY Layer (Enablement System)

- Most often, the easiest to solve

- What You Are Managing
  - How tools interact with real human behavior and workflows.
