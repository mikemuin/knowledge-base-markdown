Here’s the “core logic” of the National Health Data Repository (NHDR) as defined by the **UHC Act (RA 11223)** and its **IRR** (there isn’t a separate standalone “NHDR law”—it’s mainly **Section 31 of RA 11223**, with NHDR mechanics spelled out in the **IRR**). ([Supreme Court E-Library](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/2/86448))

## Foundational concepts

- **Why it exists (purpose): evidence-informed policy + planning**
  - The law mandates submission of health/health-related data to **generate information for research and policymaking** (evidence-informed sectoral planning for UHC). ([Supreme Court E-Library](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/2/86448))
- **What data is covered: very broad “health and health-related data”**
  - The IRR defines it as variables/parameters tied to individual and population health and well-being, including **administrative, public health, medical, pharmaceutical, and health financing data** (not limited to claims).
- **Who must submit: basically everyone in the health ecosystem**
  - The law covers **public and private**, **national and local** “health-related entities.” ([Supreme Court E-Library](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/2/86448))
  - The IRR clarifies this includes **health care facilities**, relevant **government agencies** providing health services, and **agencies collecting health data** (and more).
- **Where data goes: PhilHealth is the receiving institution**
  - Submission is to **PhilHealth** (law), and the IRR says submission happens **through a National Health Data Repository**. ([Supreme Court E-Library](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/2/86448))
- **How it’s enforced: submission becomes a compliance requirement**
  - The IRR explicitly ties submission to **licensing and contracting arrangements** (i.e., it’s not optional if you want to operate/contract).
- **Interoperability is not optional**
  - Separate but tightly linked: **all health service providers and insurers** must maintain a health information system (ERP, HR info, EHR, e-prescription log) aligned with **DOH standards** and **uploaded regularly via interoperable systems**. ([Supreme Court E-Library](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/2/86448))
- **DOH access is built-in**
  - The law requires PhilHealth to **furnish DOH a copy** of the health data. ([Supreme Court E-Library](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/2/86448))
  - The IRR operationalizes this as DOH being given **access to the NHDR**.
- **Privacy/legal basis: “notice” replaces individual consent for submission**
  - The IRR requires entities to issue a **proper notice** to clients that collected data will be submitted to NHDR **in lieu of informed consent**, and that use must comply with **RA 10173 (Data Privacy Act)**.
  - The law’s HIS requirement also explicitly anchors on patient privacy/confidentiality under the Data Privacy Act. ([Supreme Court E-Library](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/2/86448))
- **Public-record concept (with legal limits)**
  - The law says health/nutrition/demographic administrative and survey data generated using **public funds** are **public records** and should be accessible unless prohibited by law. ([Supreme Court E-Library](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/2/86448))
- **Noncompliance has penalties**
  - The IRR penalizes **failure to submit health and health-related data to PhilHealth** with a fine (₱5,000 to ₱20,000 per count, after due notice and hearing).
