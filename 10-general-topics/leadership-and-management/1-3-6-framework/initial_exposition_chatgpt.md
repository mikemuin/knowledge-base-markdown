# The 1-3-6 Framework
## A Temporal Thinking Discipline for Leadership, Management, and Strategic Planning

> **Core idea:** The 1-3-6 Framework is a *time-horizon* lens for creating plans and assessing decisions—originally framed as **1 day / 3 weeks / 6 months**, now refined as a **symbolic, executive-grade thinking discipline** that works across leadership domains.

---

## 1) Executive Summary

Senior leaders face recurring tensions:
- **Urgency vs. strategy**
- **Quick wins vs. transformation**
- **Operational noise vs. systemic impact**

The 1-3-6 Framework resolves these tensions by requiring leaders to evaluate decisions across **three increasing depths of consequence**:

- **1 = Signal (Immediate interpretation)**
- **3 = Behavioral shift (Execution friction and adaptation)**
- **6 = Structural consequence (Capabilities, dependencies, advantage)**

This prevents:
- Over-indexing on short-term optics (Layer 1)
- Ignoring the execution “messy middle” (Layer 3)
- Making long-term bets without near-term traction or realism (Layer 6)

---

## 2) The Core Architecture

### The Three Layers (Symbolic)

| Layer | Temporal Meaning       | Organizational Layer   | Executive Question                               |
| ----: | ---------------------- | ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| **1** | Immediate signal       | Perception & direction | **What does this signal?**                       |
| **3** | Behavioral adjustment  | Execution & friction   | **What behaviors will shift (and what breaks)?** |
| **6** | Structural consequence | Capability & advantage | **What becomes embedded or irreversible?**       |

### The Foundational Thesis

Every meaningful leadership decision creates impact across:
1. **Interpretation** (what people think it means)
2. **Adaptation** (how people change behavior in response)
3. **Institutionalization** (what becomes part of the system)

**Weak leadership** manages interpretation.
**Average leadership** manages adaptation.
**Elite leadership** anticipates institutionalization.

## 3) Why This Works for Top Management

### Leadership psychology it addresses
Executives often:
- Speak in abstraction (“strategy”)
- React to daily pressure (“firefighting”)
- Under-map the mid-term consequences (the *real* battleground)

The **Layer 3 window** is the decisive zone:
- It reveals adoption friction, resistance patterns, workflow reality
- It’s where most change initiatives succeed or fail
- It’s where “good strategy” dies from execution blindness

### Biases it corrects
- **Recency bias**: overweighting immediate effects (Layer 1)
- **Execution blindness**: underestimating friction and workarounds (Layer 3)
- **Structural myopia**: failing to see what becomes permanent (Layer 6)

## 4) Primary Use Case: A Thinking Discipline (Anchor)

### The core discipline (usable in any exec discussion)
Before committing to a decision, leaders ask:

1. **Signal**
   - What message does this send (internally/externally)?
   - What precedent does it set?
   - What expectations does it create?

3. **Behavior**
   - What behaviors must change for this to work?
   - What friction, resistance, or workarounds appear?
   - What execution strain does this introduce?

6. **Structure**
   - What will become embedded (process, dependency, culture)?
   - What capability do we gain or lose?
   - What advantage (or vulnerability) becomes durable?

### The meta-shift
Most leaders manage events.
The 1-3-6 mindset shifts leadership toward **consequence design**:
> “What becomes reinforced because of this decision?”

---

## 5) Planning Application: 1-3-6 as Staged Impact

When used to create plans (not just assess decisions), it becomes a staged model:

### 1 = Activation (Proof of movement)
- What action demonstrates progress immediately?
- What signal do we send to mobilize attention?
- What decision unlocks momentum?

### 3 = Traction (Reality of execution)
- What measurable shift should be underway?
- What resistance will surface?
- What must become routine?

### 6 = Transformation (Capability and advantage)
- What new capability now exists?
- What dependency is reduced?
- What competitive or structural edge is created?

**Purpose:** Turn vague strategy into an impact path with checkpoints.

---

## 6) Decision & IT/System Assessment Application

For any decision, technology, system, or investment:

### 1 (Signal)
- Disruption and immediate productivity shocks
- Morale and stakeholder reaction
- Operational interruption / “go-live” risk
- Reputation and narrative impact

### 3 (Behavior)
- Adoption friction and training burden
- Workarounds, shadow processes, parallel systems
- Productivity dips or rebounds
- Stakeholder tensions and process breakdowns

### 6 (Structure)
- Data integrity and decision velocity
- Cost structure changes (opex/capex, support load)
- Vendor dependence and lock-in
- Durable capability creation or strategic constraint

**Key insight:**
- Most system failures reveal themselves in **Layer 3**.
- Most system regrets become undeniable in **Layer 6**.

## 9) Advanced Enhancements (Optional but Powerful)

### 1) Impact Gradient
Not every decision should strongly affect all layers. Classify decisions by dominant layer:
- **Signal-heavy**: narrative, announcements, market messaging
- **Behavior-heavy**: process changes, reorganizations, operational redesign
- **Structure-heavy**: platforms, operating models, incentive systems, long-term bets

**Benefit:** avoids overengineering trivial decisions.

### 2) Reversibility Index (Strategic Gravity)
Ask:
- Is this reversible at **Layer 1**?
- Painful at **Layer 3**?
- Nearly irreversible at **Layer 6**?

**Benefit:** improves risk-calibrated decision making.

### 3) Stakeholder Axis (Matrix Expansion)
For each layer, assess impact by stakeholder:
- Customers
- Employees
- Investors
- Partners
- Regulators

**Benefit:** turns 1-3-6 into a robust governance and board discussion tool.

------

## Core Principle

**Every meaningful decision must create value on three horizons simultaneously:**

| Horizon      | Function       | Question                      | Failure Mode         |
| ------------ | -------------- | ----------------------------- | -------------------- |
| **1 Day**    | Activation     | What changes immediately?     | Planning theater     |
| **3 Weeks**  | Adoption       | What becomes normal behavior? | Pilot purgatory      |
| **6 Months** | Transformation | What becomes irreversible?    | Cosmetic improvement |

The framework works because **organizational impact is temporal, not conceptual.**

A system that cannot produce results in **1 day** is theoretical.

A system that cannot stabilize in **3 weeks** is fragile.

A system that cannot reshape the environment in **6 months** is noise.

------

## Why These Intervals Work

### 1 Day = Proof of Motion

This is the **credibility horizon.**

Within 24 hours you should see:

- A decision executed
- A process started
- A visible artifact created
- A constraint removed

Not results — **movement.**

If nothing changes in 1 day:

- The plan is not executable
- Authority is unclear
- Ownership is missing

The 1-day horizon tests **organizational friction.**

It answers:

> Can we actually move?

------

### 3 Weeks = Behavioral Shift

Three weeks is roughly **two operational cycles** in most organizations.

Enough time to see:

- Habits forming
- Resistance emerging
- Process weaknesses exposed
- Early metrics stabilizing

This horizon answers:

> Does the system absorb the change?

At 3 weeks:

- Workarounds appear
- Training gaps surface
- Informal power structures react

If progress collapses before 3 weeks:

- The change was never integrated into real workflows.

This is where most initiatives die.

------

### 6 Months = Structural Impact

Six months is long enough for:

- Budgets to adjust
- Policies to change
- Systems to integrate
- Performance metrics to shift
- Staff expectations to reset

This horizon answers:

> Does the organization become different?

After 6 months:

- Old methods should feel obsolete
- New methods should feel normal

If after 6 months nothing structural changed:

The initiative was **cosmetic.**

## The Real Strength: Dual Use

Your strongest insight is that **1-3-6 works both as a planning tool and an evaluation tool.**

That is rare.

### Planning Mode

Before approval:

Ask:

**1 Day**

- What will physically change tomorrow?
- Who will do it?
- What artifact proves it happened?

**3 Weeks**

- What behavior becomes routine?
- What metric moves?
- What friction emerges?

**6 Months**

- What becomes permanent?
- What cost disappears?
- What risk disappears?

If leaders cannot answer these:

The plan is fiction.

------

### Evaluation Mode

After implementation:

Ask:

**1 Day**

- Did anything actually start?

**3 Weeks**

- Are people working differently?

**6 Months**

- Did the organization evolve?

This avoids fake success metrics.

------

## Application to IT Systems

This is where the framework becomes powerful.

Most IT decisions fail because they are evaluated only at the **6-month level.**

Instead:

### Example: New Ticketing System

**1 Day**

- First tickets entered
- Users logged in
- Support team trained

Evidence:

- Real tickets processed

If this fails:

The system is unusable.

------

**3 Weeks**

- Users submit tickets instead of email
- Average response time stabilizes
- Workflows understood

Evidence:

- Email volume drops

If this fails:

The system is not adopted.

------

**6 Months**

- Historical data usable
- Reporting trusted
- Staffing decisions informed

Evidence:

- Management decisions based on system data

If this fails:

The system is irrelevant.

## Refinement Suggestion (Important)

Your framework becomes much stronger if you define **what counts as impact.**

Otherwise, it becomes subjective.

Impact should fall into **four categories:**

1. **Speed**
   - Time saved
   - Cycle reduction
2. **Quality**
   - Error reduction
   - Reliability increase
3. **Cost**
   - Direct savings
   - Avoided labor
4. **Risk**
   - Failure prevention
   - Compliance improvement

Every 1-3-6 plan should show at least one of these.

Otherwise, it has no impact.
