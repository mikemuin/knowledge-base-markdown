# Clinical Workflow Hierarchy: Comprehensive Classification System

## 1. Information Management
**Primary Function:** Management and handling of all clinical data and documentation
**Key Purpose:** Ensure accurate, accessible, and complete clinical information for decision-making

### 1.1 Reviewing
**Definition:** Systematic examination of clinical information and data
**Core Activities:**
- Reading patient charts and history
- Examining lab and diagnostic results
- Analyzing trends in clinical data
- Studying documentation from other providers
**Workflow Impact:** Forms the foundation for clinical decision-making

### 1.2 Documenting
**Definition:** Creation and maintenance of clinical records
**Core Activities:**
- Writing clinical notes and assessments
- Recording procedures and interventions
- Creating care plans and orders
- Maintaining ongoing progress documentation
**Workflow Impact:** Ensures care continuity and legal compliance

### 1.3 Acknowledging
**Definition:** Formal verification and sign-off of clinical information
**Core Activities:**
- Signing off on test results
- Confirming receipt of critical information
- Verifying completion of orders
- Accepting transfers of care
**Workflow Impact:** Maintains accountability and closes communication loops

## 2. Clinical Decision-Making
**Primary Function:** Analysis, judgment, and determination of clinical actions
**Key Purpose:** Convert clinical information into actionable care plans

### 2.1 Evaluating
**Definition:** Assessment and analysis of clinical situations
**Core Activities:**
- Assessing patient condition
- Analyzing treatment responses
- Determining intervention effectiveness
- Judging clinical risks and benefits
**Workflow Impact:** Drives clinical decision-making process

### 2.2 Planning
**Definition:** Development of structured care approaches
**Core Activities:**
- Creating treatment strategies
- Developing care plans
- Setting clinical goals
- Designing intervention approaches
**Workflow Impact:** Provides framework for organized care delivery

### 2.3 Ordering
**Definition:** Initiation of clinical actions through formal requests
**Core Activities:**
- Prescribing medications
- Ordering diagnostic tests
- Requesting consultations
- Initiating clinical protocols
**Workflow Impact:** Translates decisions into actionable steps

## 3. Care Delivery
**Primary Function:** Execution and monitoring of clinical care
**Key Purpose:** Implement and track clinical interventions

### 3.1 Carrying Out
**Definition:** Direct execution of clinical activities
**Core Activities:**
- Performing procedures
- Administering treatments
- Conducting examinations
- Implementing interventions
**Workflow Impact:** Represents direct patient care actions

### 3.2 Monitoring
**Definition:** Ongoing surveillance of patient status
**Core Activities:**
- Tracking vital signs
- Observing symptoms
- Following treatment responses
- Conducting regular assessments
**Workflow Impact:** Provides continuous feedback on care effectiveness

## 4. Care Coordination
**Primary Function:** Organization and synchronization of care activities
**Key Purpose:** Ensure integrated and efficient care delivery

### 4.1 Communicating
**Definition:** Information exchange between all care participants
**Core Activities:**
- Conducting team discussions
- Providing patient education
- Performing handoffs
- Updating families
**Workflow Impact:** Ensures shared understanding and care continuity

### 4.2 Coordinating
**Definition:** Management of care logistics and resources
**Core Activities:**
- Managing care transitions
- Arranging services and resources
- Orchestrating team activities
- Planning complex care delivery
**Workflow Impact:** Optimizes care efficiency and integration

## Workflow Integration Principles

### 1. Vertical Integration
- Information Management supports all higher levels
- Clinical Decision-Making directs Care Delivery
- Care Coordination spans across all levels
- Each level builds upon and informs others

### 2. Horizontal Integration
- Categories within each level interact continuously
- Information flows between parallel activities
- Activities often occur simultaneously
- Coordination happens at each level

### 3. Feedback Loops
- Monitoring information feeds back to Decision-Making
- Outcomes influence future Planning
- Communication affects all categories
- Documentation captures all activities

### 4. Common Workflows Examples

**New Patient Admission:**
1. Information Management
   - Review referral information
   - Document initial assessment
   - Acknowledge handoff
2. Clinical Decision-Making
   - Evaluate current status
   - Plan initial care approach
   - Order admission interventions
3. Care Delivery
   - Conduct initial assessment
   - Monitor response to interventions
4. Care Coordination
   - Communicate with team
   - Coordinate required services

**Acute Status Change:**
1. Information Management
   - Review current data
   - Document changes
   - Acknowledge alerts
2. Clinical Decision-Making
   - Evaluate situation
   - Plan response
   - Order interventions
3. Care Delivery
   - Implement urgent care
   - Monitor response
4. Care Coordination
   - Alert necessary providers
   - Coordinate emergency response
