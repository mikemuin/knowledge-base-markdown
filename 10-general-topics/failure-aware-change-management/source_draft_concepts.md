# Source Draft Concepts

## Core Concepts

- Change management is the management of failure.
- Change management is the systematic identification, containment, and adaptation to failure across people, process, and technology before it compounds into irreversible loss.
- Change management assumes failure is inevitable and designs the organization to detect, contain, and adapt to it in real time.

## Foundational Reframe

- **Traditional frameworks:** Adoption is achievable. Resistance is the exception to be managed.
- **This framework:** Failure is the default. Success is engineered.
- **Subtext:** That is not pessimism. It is the discipline that traditional optimism consistently fails to supply.
- **Notes:** This is the reframe that earns the room's attention. Don't soften it. The provocation is the point.

## The Silent Failure Problem

### The Failures That Make No Noise

- In most project environments, failure is visible: a missed deadline shows on the plan, a budget overrun shows in the report, and a system outage triggers an alert.
- In Health IT change management, the most consequential failures do not announce themselves. A stakeholder who was supportive in month one drifts into disengagement by month four; no alert fires. A clinical champion starts to withdraw; no report captures it. Data begins flowing incorrectly between two systems that both appear to be working; no dashboard turns red.
- Detection cannot be passive. It must be actively designed with instruments, cadences, and named owners whose specific job is to look for things that are not announcing themselves.

### Skipped Planning Question

The question most commonly skipped in project planning:

> How will we know if something is going wrong before it becomes a crisis?

It is skipped because it is uncomfortable. It requires looking for evidence that the project is not working.

## Deeper Breakdown

### 1. Failure Is Not an Exception; It Is the Default Condition of Change

Every change introduces:

- Misalignment: people do not interpret the change the same way.
- Friction: processes do not fit reality.
- Breakdown: technology does not behave as expected.

Change management exists because initial plans are wrong when they come into contact with reality.

The mindset: expect failure; be ready.

### The Critical Distinction

Most teams treat failure as:

- **An outcome:** something that happens at the end.

This framework defines failure as:

- **A continuous signal stream:** something observed during execution.

That is the leverage.

### 2. A Key Task of Change Management Is Early Detection

You are not managing change. You are managing:

- Weak signals of resistance.
- Small process deviations.
- Minor system inconsistencies.

Handled early: cheap.

Ignored: exponential cost.

### 3. It Is an Active Implementation System, Not Just a Communication Plan

Most organizations reduce change management to communications and training. That is surface-level.

Real change management:

- Prevents localized failures from spreading.
- Isolates issues before they cascade across teams.
- Creates buffers: fallbacks, redundancies, and escalation paths.

### 4. It Is an Adaptive System, Not a Control System

You do not enforce change into existence.

You:

- Continuously update assumptions.
- Adjust rollout based on real-world feedback.
- Trade speed versus stability in real time.

## Strategic Reframe

You are not managing change.

You are building an **organizational immune system**:

- Detect threats early.
- Isolate them.
- Adapt and strengthen.

Without that, every transformation becomes fragile.

## Operational Requirements

You need two things:

1. A **dashboard that forces visibility of failure**.
2. A **live simulation to train leaders to respond correctly under pressure**.

You are building a **Failure Management Overlay on PPT (People, Process, Technology)** as an execution system, not just as a concept.

## The Six Considerations / Questions

At any point in the change lifecycle, you are managing six things simultaneously:

1. **Identify Success Criteria:** What does success look like for this?
2. **Assumptions:** What things are we thinking should be in place, and what are given?
3. **Identifying Risk:** Where do we expect to fail? What will fail?
4. **Detection:** How do we first detect it? How do we know?
5. **Prevention:** What do we need to do now to avoid that?
6. **Respond:** What do we need to do when that happens?

## The PPT Overlay

The PPT Overlay allows identification of project components where failure can happen.

## 1. People: Failure in Alignment and Behavior

### Expected Failure Modes

- Misunderstanding of the why.
- Passive resistance: nodding, no adoption.
- Active resistance: pushback, slowdown.
- Loss of trust in leadership.
- Reversion to old behaviors.

### Failure Modes

- **Flying Blind:** You do not know who matters, where they stand, or what authority they hold.
- **Influential Opponent:** The person everyone follows is working against you.
- **Owner in Name Only:** Someone holds the accountability but does not act on it. This is more dangerous than no owner at all.
- **Not Enough Advocates:** The right advocates are not in the right places.
- **Too Many Fence-Sitters:** The majority are still waiting to see who wins.
- **Resistance Mismanagement:** Pushback is being handled the wrong way.
- **Stale Situation:** Your picture of the room is out of date.

### Detection Signals

- Drop in engagement or participation.
- Increase in side conversations or complaints.
- Low training retention or repeated questions.
- Managers giving inconsistent messages.
- Emergence of workarounds instead of adoption.

### Containment Actions

- Immediate clarification loops: targeted conversations, not mass communications.
- Deploy frontline managers as interpreters, not messengers.
- Isolate resistant groups and engage directly.
- Reinforce why tied to local impact, not corporate narrative.

### Adaptation Moves

- Adjust messaging based on real objections.
- Change incentives if behavior is not shifting.
- Retrain or replace key influencers if misalignment persists.

## 2. Process: Failure in Execution Flow

### Expected Failure Modes

- Designed process does not match real-world conditions.
- Bottlenecks emerge in unexpected steps.
- Shadow processes or unofficial workarounds appear.
- Increased cycle time or delays.
- Role confusion and duplication of effort.

### Failure Modes

- **Getting the Current Picture Wrong:** New process designed against a false picture of how things actually work today.
- **Designed for Ideal Conditions:** Workflow works in planning, but falls apart in real operating conditions.
- **Stuck Between Old and New:** Old and new processes run simultaneously until the hybrid becomes the new normal. This is the organizing risk.
- **Missing How Work Actually Gets Done:** Informal practices disrupted by the new process with no plan to replace them.
- **Process Working for Champions, Not for Everyone:** Inconsistent execution hidden below headline compliance numbers.
- **Process Works but Produces No Improvement:** Compliance achieved; outcomes flat.
- **Sliding Back to the Old Way of Working:** Future state erodes under pressure after the project closes.

### Detection Signals

- Tasks piling up at specific stages.
- Increase in exception handling.
- Teams skipping steps to get things done.
- Frequent escalations or rework.
- Time-to-complete drifting beyond baseline.

### Containment Actions

- Freeze and isolate failing process segments.
- Allow controlled deviations; do not force broken compliance.
- Rapid process huddles with actual operators, not designers.
- Reassign resources to unblock critical path.

### Adaptation Moves

- Redesign workflows based on observed behavior.
- Simplify steps; remove theoretical nice-to-haves.
- Re-clarify ownership and decision rights.
- Update SOPs in real time, not post-mortem.

## 3. Technology: Failure in Systems and Tools

### Expected Failure Modes

- System bugs or instability.
- Integration failures between tools.
- Poor usability leading to low adoption.
- Mismatch between system design and actual workflow.
- Data inconsistency or loss.

### Failure Modes

- **Wrong System Selected:** The system chosen does not fit how staff actually work.
- **Access and Infrastructure Failures:** Staff cannot reliably access the system where the work happens.
- **Wrong Information, Real Consequences:** The system produces unreliable or incorrect information. This is the highest patient safety risk.
- **Staff Not Using the System:** Staff avoid the system and find other ways to get the work done.
- **System Does Not Improve the Work:** The system is running, but the expected improvements are not happening.
- **Decline After Launch:** The system works at launch and gradually deteriorates.

### Detection Signals

- Spike in support tickets.
- Users abandoning system or reverting to manual work.
- Error rates increasing.
- Latency or performance complaints.
- Data discrepancies across systems.

### Containment Actions

- Rollback or limit feature exposure; do not let failure scale.
- Create manual fallback paths.
- Prioritize fixes based on business impact, not technical severity.
- Stand up rapid-response support that is visible and accessible.

### Adaptation Moves

- Reprioritize development roadmap based on usage reality.
- Improve UX where friction is highest.
- Adjust integrations to reflect actual process flows.
- Retrain users, but only after fixing root usability issues.

## The Integrator Layer

### The Fourth Layer

The real breakdown is not in PPT individually. It is in **their interaction**.

### Cross-Domain Failure Patterns

- Tech issue becomes blamed on people: they are not adopting.
- Process gap becomes patched with manual work, and technology becomes irrelevant.
- People resistance exposes process flaws leadership ignored.

## How to Use the Framework

1. Identify the different elements of the PPT overlay in the project.
2. Ask the six questions:
   1. **Identify Success Criteria:** What does success look like for this?
   2. **Assumptions:** What things are we thinking should be in place, and what are given?
   3. **Identifying Risk:** Where do we expect to fail? What will fail?
   4. **Detection:** How do we first detect it? How do we know?
   5. **Prevention:** What do we need to do now to avoid that?
   6. **Respond:** What do we need to do when that happens?
3. Implement changes to the Project Implementation Plan:
   1. Preventive Measure.
   2. Detection Mechanisms.
   3. Response Mechanisms.

## Ideas to Implement the Framework

### 1. Failure Management Dashboard: PPT-Aligned

This is not a status report. It is a **decision system**.

### Structure

Use weekly review, or real-time review for critical projects.

#### A. Executive Snapshot: Top Layer

- **Overall Change Health:** Green / Yellow / Red, based on failure velocity, not completion percentage.
- **Top 3 Active Failures:** Tagged as People / Process / Tech.
- **Failure Trend:** Increasing / Stable / Decreasing.
- **Critical Path at Risk:** Yes / No.

#### B. Failure Register: Core Engine

Each failure gets logged like this:

- **Failure ID:** F-017
- **Domain:** Process
- **Description:** Approval step causing 3-day delay vs 1-day target.

**Signal Type:**

- Leading: early warning.
- Lagging: already impacting output.

**Severity:**

- Low: localized.
- Medium: cross-team impact.
- High: critical path risk.

**Detection Signal:**

- Cycle time increase from 1 to 3 days.

**Containment Action Now:**

- Temporary bypass for low-risk approvals.

**Adaptation Decision Next:**

- Redesign approval logic: threshold-based auto-approval.

**Owner:** Single accountable person.

**Status:** Open / Contained / Resolved.

#### C. Decision Log: Leadership Accountability

Every cycle forces decisions:

- What are we **stopping**?
- What are we **changing immediately**?
- What are we **accepting as risk for now**?

No decision means silent failure expansion.

## The Real Outcome

You are training leaders to:

- See failure early without denial.
- Act without waiting for certainty.
- Balance stability versus momentum.
- Coordinate across PPT, not in silos.

## Final Integration

The full system:

- **Philosophy:** Expect failure.
- **Framework:** PPT failure overlay.
- **Tool:** Failure dashboard.
- **Capability:** Simulation-based leadership training.
